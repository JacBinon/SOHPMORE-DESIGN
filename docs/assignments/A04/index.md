# A4 – Motor Mount

# Objective

The objective of this assignment is to design and analyze a motor mount for a 24 V DC gear motor while applying principles of beam bending, stress analysis, and deflection. The motor mount will be divided into two main features, and each feature will be analyzed as a beam to determine the required cross-sectional geometry. The design will first be evaluated based on yield strength using a safety factor of 3 and then checked to ensure that the maximum deflection does not exceed 0.30 mm at the free end.

ABS, PETG, or PLA will be selected as the material based on its mechanical properties and suitability for the application. Reasonable assumptions will be made to simplify the analysis while still representing the behavior of the actual mount. The calculated dimensions will then be used to develop a parametric CAD model of the motor mount. Additional features, such as ribs or gussets, will be considered to improve the stiffness of the design and reduce deflection.

Throughout the project, the design process will be documented through free-body diagrams, hand calculations, sketches, CAD models, design changes, and research into existing motor mount designs. The final design will demonstrate how analytical calculations can be used to determine practical dimensions and guide the development of a functional motor mount.

I decided to define the length and width of the feature and only solve for the thickness. This simplifies the calculations by leaving only one variable to solve for. It also ensures that the motor will fit and have enough room to mount. I chose a width of 37 mm because this placed the mounting holes for the motor 6 mm from the edge. I chose a distance of 6 mm because it is roughly two times the hole diameter, which is a good rule of thumb I have picked up from parts I have designed in previous projects.

Now that I had defined what I needed to solve for, I needed to further define the problem with a free-body diagram. Per the directions, I defined Feature 1 as a cantilever beam with one end fixed and the other receiving the force.

With this design, Feature 1 takes up a large portion of the top surface of Feature 2. This became somewhat of a problem because there was no room for the forward two bolt holes. Because of this, I decided to make these blind threaded holes. This would allow the mount to still be mounted and prevent the need for nuts or other additional hardware during mounting.

Using threads like this does limit the mount by requiring the bolts to be driven upward from the bottom. However, with the motor installed, this is the only direction from which the holes are accessible anyway.

I chose to thread these holes as M4 x 0.7 with a thread depth of 10 mm. To ensure that the plastic could withstand the required load without tearing out, I referenced an online thread pullout calculator. This calculated that each bolt threaded 10 mm deep into ABS could hold 1150 N of force.

Our maximum load on the motor is only 300 N. Therefore, each bolt would be almost enough to hold the mount and maintain our 3x safety factor. With four of these bolts installed, this gives us a maximum thread pullout load of 4600 N, or over a 15x safety factor.
